The Holiday Season Is Doing Something to Your Body. Here's What It Is.

December 1, 2026 By Daniel Corbin, DNP, APRN, FNP-C 7 min read

Every year around this time, I start seeing a pattern in my patients. Energy is low. Sleep is off. Anxiety is up. Weight is creeping. And everyone assumes it's just the holidays.

It's not just the holidays. It's cortisol.

What Actually Happens to Your Body This Time of Year

The holiday season is one of the most reliable cortisol triggers of the year. Financial pressure, family dynamics, packed schedules, disrupted routines, less sleep, more alcohol, more sugar. Each one of those things raises cortisol on its own. Stack them together for six weeks and your body is running in a low-grade stress state almost continuously.

Cortisol is your main stress hormone. In small doses it's useful. It gets you up in the morning, helps you respond to challenges, and gives you energy when you need it. But when it stays elevated for weeks at a time, it starts causing real problems.

Here's what chronically high cortisol actually does:

  • Makes it harder to fall asleep and stay asleep, even when you're exhausted
  • Raises your blood sugar, which leads to cravings, energy crashes, and weight gain around your midsection
  • Suppresses your immune system, which is why so many people get sick in December
  • Increases inflammation throughout your body
  • Makes anxiety and irritability worse, which makes the stress worse, which raises cortisol more

It's a cycle. And most people don't realize they're in it until January, when they feel completely wrecked.

The Stress You Don't Notice Is Still Stress

One thing I want people to understand is that cortisol doesn't only go up when you feel stressed. It goes up in response to anything your body perceives as a demand.

Poor sleep raises cortisol. Skipping meals raises cortisol. Eating a lot of sugar raises cortisol. Drinking alcohol raises cortisol, even if the drink felt relaxing in the moment. Being overscheduled raises cortisol even if you enjoy what you're doing.

This is why the holidays are so hard on the body even for people who genuinely love the season. It's not about whether you're enjoying yourself. It's about what you're asking your body to handle.

What You Can Actually Do About It

You don't have to opt out of the holidays to protect your health. But a few intentional choices make a real difference.

Protect your sleep above everything else. Cortisol and sleep have a two-way relationship. High cortisol disrupts sleep, and poor sleep raises cortisol. If you let sleep slip for six weeks straight, everything else gets harder. Set a floor, even during the busy season, and try not to go below it.

Eat before you go. Showing up to holiday parties hungry leads to blood sugar spikes and poor choices that raise cortisol for hours afterward. A small meal with protein before you go keeps your blood sugar stable and makes it easier to drink less and eat more intentionally.

Be selective about your yes. Every obligation you add is a demand on your nervous system. Some of them are worth it. Some of them aren't. This time of year, saying no to one thing is an act of self-care that has real physiological benefits.

Move your body, even briefly. Exercise is one of the most effective ways to burn off excess cortisol. It doesn't have to be a long workout. A 20-minute walk does more for your stress hormones than most people realize.

Watch the alcohol. I'm not saying don't drink. I'm saying understand what it does. Alcohol feels like it relaxes you because it temporarily suppresses your nervous system. But as it metabolizes, it raises cortisol, disrupts your sleep architecture, and leaves you more anxious the next day. If you're already running high on stress, alcohol makes it worse, not better.

What January Looks Like When You Do This

Most people come into the new year feeling depleted, inflamed, and behind. Their cortisol has been elevated for weeks, their sleep is a mess, and their body is in recovery mode.

It doesn't have to be that way. The holidays can be full and joyful and still not wreck your health. It just takes a little awareness of what's happening under the surface.

If you want to come into January with your health actually intact, start paying attention to your cortisol now. Your body will thank you in February.
